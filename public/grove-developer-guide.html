2. Getting Started

1
Classify before you build
One question determines your tier: does the app have a backend? No backend = Tier 1, regardless of who uses it. Backend + internal only = Tier 2. Backend + external or mixed = Tier 3.
2
Register in Grove early
Add your project as soon as the idea is real. Log in at grove.sprout.solutions → click Add Plant. Fill in the name, description, problem space, tools, and data sources.
3
Fill in the Technical tab
Answer the classification questions to lock in your tier. Then complete the per-tier checklist (hosting, auth, database, repo). Mandatory before any stage change.
4
Declare your approver & secure IS/Execom sign-off
Every project needs a named approver — your IS contact or an Execom member. Record the name and email in the Overview tab. Optional at Sprout — mandatory before advancing to Blooming.
5
Submit for release review (Tier 2 & 3)
When ready to go live, click Submit for Release Review. Belle Asis reviews your classification, data sources, and tech stack. Tier 3 gets a Jira DevOps ticket. Wait for approval before advancing.
6
Move stages & go live
Advance one stage at a time — Sprout → Growing → Blooming → Thriving. Click the stage card on your project overview. Blooming = live. Thriving = full Sprout portfolio product.

3. Classify Your Project

Does it have a backend?
Any code running outside the browser — APIs, servers, data pipelines, scheduled jobs, databases. No backend = Tier 1, no matter who uses it.
Who uses it? (backend projects only)
Only relevant if the project has a backend. Internal = Sprout employees only → Tier 2. External or Both = clients, partners, public, or mixed → Tier 3.

4. Dev Standards

Authentication

Tier 1 — no authentication required
No login or session management needed for static tools, regardless of who accesses them.
Tier 2 — internal auth is sufficient
Sprout Google email is the standard for internal apps. No Keycloak required at this tier.
Tier 3 — Keycloak is required
External-facing apps must use Keycloak. Coordinate with Coleen Bartido (DevOps) before setting up auth. Read the Keycloak setup guide first.

Database

✓ Preferred
  • Supabase — preferred for most app databases
  • PostgreSQL / Azure SQL for complex needs
  • Move DB to Sprout ownership at go-live (Tier 2/3)
  • No credentials hardcoded in the repo
✗ Avoid in production
  • Airtable — not suitable for production
  • Google Sheets — fine for prototypes only
  • Personal Supabase / Firebase at go-live
  • Secrets stored in plain text / committed to git

Hosting

Tier 1 — Markup
Upload directly to Markup. No deployment infrastructure required.
Tier 2 & 3 — Personal repo is fine during development
You can develop in your own personal repository. However, before going live, all assets — repository, hosting, database, configs, and secrets — must be transferred to the Company Repository (Sprout Vercel or Sprout Azure). Raise a DevOps ticket for Coleen Bartido once IS/Execom approval is in place.
→
Development — personal GitHub repo, personal Vercel, local DB: all fine.
Go-live (Blooming) — everything must move to Sprout-owned accounts. IS/Execom approval must come before the DevOps ticket.

5. Register in Grove

1
Log in to Grove
Go to grove.sprout.solutions. Sign in with your Sprout Google account (@sprout.ph or @sproutsolutions.io). Your country is auto-set and cannot be changed.
2
Click "Add Plant"
From the Garden view, click Add Plant. Fill in: project name, description, problem space, department, tools used, and data sources.

Answer all 5 security questions — tier is computed automatically. Then fill the per-tier checklist that appears.

Declare your approver
In the Overview tab, find the Approver section. Enter the name and email of your IS contact or Execom member. Click Send Approval Request.

7. Tips & Gotchas

Tip 1
Classify before you build, not after
One question tells you most of what you need: does it have a backend? No backend = Tier 1, no matter who uses it. This saves rearchitecting later.
Tip 2
Register early — stages are just a label
You don't need to finish building before registering. Add a plant as soon as the idea is real. It lets you track progress and start the approval clock early.
Tip 3
Secure your approver during Sprout
IS/Execom approval is required before any DevOps ticket. If you wait until Growing, you might block yourself from Blooming. Get the approval early.
Tip 4
Read the Keycloak guide before asking Coleen
There's an existing Keycloak setup guide. Work through it first. Coleen Bartido is available for escalations — not setup walkthroughs.
Tip 5
Supabase for databases — not Airtable
Airtable and Sheets are fine for prototyping. For a production tool used by real users, use Supabase. It avoids a painful migration at go-live review.
Tip 6
If it's used by 3+ people, it belongs in Grove
Any internal tool accessed by more than 3 people, or that connects to a company system, must be registered. Don't wait to be asked.
Tip 7
A rejection is not the end
If your release review is rejected, read the comment carefully, fix the flagged issue, and resubmit. The review queue moves quickly once resolved.
